## How one source finding becomes a proposed feature

**Source detail:** J01 asks designers to create trustworthy, user-centered AI experiences and map user needs into product logic.

**My inference:** If someone cannot tell whether an ideation tool is waiting, has no result, or needs a better prompt, they may not know how to continue. This is a hypothesis; the employer posting does not make a claim about Idea Thinker users.

**Proposed design:** In my *Idea Thinker* case study, show a clear empty state, a loading state, and an unhelpful-response state with a visible way to revise or retry.

**User story:** As someone trying to develop an idea, I want to understand the response state and what I can do next so I can continue without guessing what happened.

**Planned check — not run yet:** Once those states are in the prototype, I will ask five peers to try three tasks without hints: start from no result, identify what is happening while waiting, and recover from an unhelpful response. I will record task completion, whether each person identifies the state and next action, approximate time, and any confusion. My proposed early signal is at least four of five people completing each task without help. This is my proposed check, not a course quota, employer requirement, or observed result. If people miss the next action, I will revise the state/action and ask the reviewer to retry the same tasks.

This check can tell me whether the interaction is understandable to these participants in this prototype. It cannot prove broad user needs, hiring outcomes, or job readiness.

## My next portfolio steps

1. **Problem framing:** Write a one-page *Idea Thinker* brief that marks “student and indie creators” as a target-audience hypothesis, states the blank-page problem I want to examine, and compares three non-AI ways to begin.
2. **Interaction proof:** Refine two modes first—Guided MCQ and Movie Twist—instead of promising all modes at once. Add empty, loading, unhelpful-result, and recovery states.
3. **Reusable design decisions:** Build a small Figma component set for the chosen flow. Audit touch targets and contrast and report the actual audit findings; do not assume guideline compliance.
4. **Prototype and review:** If the flow is understandable, make a small interactive prototype. Clearly label simulated AI responses if there is no model connected, then run the planned peer tasks and record real outcomes.

The scope is deliberately staged: establish the problem and interaction before adding a live AI integration. If time is limited, I will keep the two core modes and document the limitation rather than presenting unfinished states as complete.
