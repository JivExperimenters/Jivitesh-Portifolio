## 2. Evidence is not the same as inference

### Directly observable in the postings
- **National & Local AI Design Alignment (`J01` & `J03`):** Both TikTok (`J01`) and Notion NYC (`J03`) explicitly ask for **Figma fluency** paired with **AI coding/design tools (`ChatGPT`, `Claude`, `Cursor`, `Claude Code`)** to prototype human-AI interactions.
- **Mobile & Component System Emphasis (`J01` & `J04`):** Both TikTok (`J01`) and The New York Times NYC (`J04`) emphasize iOS, Android, and web design systems.
- **Experience Barrier Contrast (`J02`):** Apple's FDE role (`J02`) requires 12+ years of full-stack production engineering, confirming that undergraduate internships (`J03`, `J04`) and graduate design tracks (`J01`) are the appropriate entry path.

### My interpretation and assumptions
- I infer that an NJIT student who can show both Figma interaction flows (*Idea Thinker*, *Offline TV*) and live deployed web prototypes (*The Reckoner*, *Only Winners Play*, *Momentum Lab*) is well positioned for NYC hybrid internships like Notion (`J03`).
- I suspect that showing empty, loading, and unhelpful-response states in *Idea Thinker* communicates stronger design reasoning than showing only happy-path screens.
- Whether 4 out of 5 classmates can actually complete the recovery flow without hints remains an **untested hypothesis (planned check)** until I run the walkthrough.

## 4. How one source finding becomes a proposed feature

- **Source detail:** Both TikTok (`J01`) and Notion NYC (`J03`) ask designers to craft intuitive human-AI product flows and map user needs into clear product logic.
- **Proposed design:** In my *Idea Thinker* case study, add three explicit system states—an **empty state**, a **loading state**, and an **unhelpful-response recovery state**—with a visible way to revise or switch modes.
- **Planned check (not run yet):** Ask five peers to complete three tasks without hints: (1) start from an empty state, (2) identify what is happening while waiting, and (3) recover from an unhelpful response. Target signal: at least 4 of 5 peers completing each task without verbal hints.
